Context

Where the diagnosis stood

GARF regresses a global SE(3) pose per fragment directly, it never performs explicit pairwise correspondence. Earlier phases confirmed the CNN's fracture mask is reliable, but every point-to-point matcher was unsuccessful. Two different ways around that wall were tried next.

SHEET 02 / 08
Path 1 · Depth-map matching

Match the fracture face as a surface, not as points

Idea: turn each fragment's fracture face into a small height field, like a terrain map, then search for the rotation and translation that make the two faces complementary, a bump on one side sitting opposite a hollow on the other.

How the height field is actually built, for one fragment:

depth(u,v) = mean height along n of the fracture points falling in cell (u,v), same idea as a terrain height map

Breaking Bad fragments break along near-planar surfaces, not textured ones

Open question, found late: the score divides by overlap area instead of rewarding it. So a poorly-overlapping hypothesis can score artificially higher than a well-fitting one. But two matching fracture faces share the same outline, by construction, that shape alone could fix the rotation even without relief, especially with only 2 fragments. Whether a score that rewards overlap/outline fit (instead of normalizing by it) would recover this signal hasn't been tested yet.

Negative with this scoring formula, not fully conclusive
SHEET 04 / 08
Path 1 · Update

A bigger sample changes the picture

Re-ran on 2,415 two-fragment objects (63% of the val set, ~9.5x the first update's sample). Two findings hold up at scale: the earlier "0%, worse than random" was a statistical fluke from too few valid pairs (N=21) and fixing the score (rewarding overlap instead of dividing by it) gives a real, growing improvement.

GT mask, everyday/val, 2,415 two-fragment objects scanned · random control stays at 0% throughout

relief (old) = -CC / overlap : a division, unstable when overlap is tiny · joint (fix) = relief_score × overlap_frac : a multiplication, safely goes to 0 instead of exploding

01 — sample size mattered

Same "relief" formula, bigger sample: Pose@30 goes from 0% (N=21) to 4.47% (N=447). The original "close, negative" conclusion was noise, not signal.

02 — the fix holds up, and gets clearer with scale

joint (27.59%) now beats the project's best prior geometric-matching result (Phase 2 gt_edge oracle, 9.6%) by almost 3x. relief itself got WORSE with more data (9.09%→4.47%), the signature of a noisy, unstable score, unlike joint which improved (21.15%→27.59%).

03 — still far from usable

27.59% means ~72% of pairs still fail at the loose 30° threshold, and ~86% fail at the stricter 15°/0.05. A real, robust signal, not a solved problem.

Reopened : positive but partial, confirmed at scale
SHEET 05 / 08
Path 2 · Compatibility classifier

Ask a simpler question: do they belong together?

Not "which point matches which", just "are these two fragments adjacent?" Pool the CNN's per-point features over each fragment's fracture surface into one vector per fragment, concatenate the pair, and classify.

3 conditions compared for the fracture mask: GT (oracle) · CNN threshold 0.3 · random points
